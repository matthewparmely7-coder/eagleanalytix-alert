# Concert series and independent workers

Run all schedules with `npm start`, or run `npm run start:history`, `npm run start:patterns`, and `npm run start:series` separately. They share DATABASE_URL. Mongo leases prevent duplicate jobs across instances. The backend must point ALERT_MONGODB_URI to this same database. No source database writes or new external services are required. Collections/indexes are initialized on worker startup.

The history worker owns snapshots and historyRevision. The pattern worker owns detection results and checks revision, event date, and timezone before saving. Changed history resets the evaluation deadline; periodic evaluation handles time eligibility and rule-version changes. Existing pattern functions remain in the alert service; the backend reads stored results.

The series worker maintains concert_series_events (metadata catalog), concert_series (versioned groups), and alert_worker_state (leases/checkpoints). It scans source tm_eventsModel plus saved tmArtistTourModel artist mappings. The initial scan and daily reconciliation cover all stored dates, including past and future; intermediate scans use updatedAt with overlap. This is discovery over the existing source catalog, not a new Ticketmaster API crawler. Missing artist identities are not guessed from event titles. A primary attraction or unambiguous saved artist-tour association is required. Cities match normalized city/state/country; no automatic metro expansion.

SERIES_GAP_DAYS (default 7) limits the gap between neighboring local performance dates, not the total run length or number of concerts. Changed artist/city/date/cancellation metadata invalidates affected groups. Whole artist/city groups are fetched once and chained in memory; overlaps preserve existing series IDs, merged/split groups retire superseded IDs. Fresh unchanged groups are skipped. Series with any future concert from today through SERIES_CANDIDATE_DAYS (default 3) are visible, with all their earlier and later members. They are labeled last known show because source coverage cannot prove that no additional dates exist. Recurring residencies can form long groups.

Series membership queues past-member histories as well as upcoming ones; old histories are anchored to their event dates. Raw TM resaleMinPrice and primaryMinPrice are retained separately for future series-price analysis. Legacy minPrice behavior is preserved for existing detectors. A failed S3 fetch does not advance historyPulledAt; Pending means no successful refresh yet.

The Last Show Effect UI displays all series by default and can filter saved demand statuses before pagination. It includes primary-price preview and resale-only charts, equivalent-time axes, event/site navigation, demand status, and an expandable evidence panel. The expanded plot shades the equivalent-time observation window and marks estimated completion of the reference performance where it falls within the chart. Primary preview never contributes to demand detection.

The source needs complete dates and artist associations. Events absent from source cannot be discovered. A full scan refreshes artist mappings and reconciles source removals. The worker only creates indexes in the alert database; ask the source database owner to provision an updatedAt index if incremental scans are slow.

Configuration is documented in .env.example. The existing DAILY_LIST_HOUR/MINUTE/TIMEZONE watchlist schedule is preserved. Existing TICK_MS and BATCH_SIZE still control history scheduling. No .env secrets are changed.

Validation: `npm test` runs offline tests and never connects to MongoDB or S3. Start workers only against the intended database; initial ingestion creates the collections and populates series.

## Last Show Effect demand evaluation

The patterns role runs a separate leased last-show-demand job against the same alert database. Only this worker computes evidence. The backend reads the saved concert_series.demand object and handles filtering/pagination; the frontend plots and displays it. Existing Supply Change/U Shape/Rocket/Low Resale rules are unchanged.

Initial configurable rules (LAST_SHOW_* in .env.example): seven-day observation, 20% primary and same-market resale inventory declines, continued decline in the final 48 hours of the observation, and resale prices holding within 5% below their earlier median for at least 24 observed hours. At least four samples and no missing/invalid values or gaps longer than 12 hours are required per measured series. Endpoint coverage must be within 12 hours. Primary zero is allowed; resale zero is treated as unavailable. TM uses resaleMinPrice only. VS and SH use their resale minimum prices and respective counts. A single complete marketplace may support a result; missing markets never count as confirmation.

Earlier shows are measured over their final seven days. Final-show comparisons align all shows to the same remaining time before start, cutting off older-show history at the equivalent stage; the final show must meet the same demand rules and have at least 90% of the earlier show's price at that stage. Only earlier shows that have reached estimated completion are references. Completion is scheduled start plus three hours, not an observed show end.

Post-performance evidence compares the six hours before the earlier show's scheduled start with the six hours after estimated completion. Both primary and same-site resale counts must decline at least 10% after completion and faster than in the equal-length before period. Median resale price must rise at least 5% and not materially undercut the pre-performance price. Incomplete post periods cannot support Last Show Effect. These thresholds are initial heuristics, not validated probabilities or proof of causation.

Statuses: insufficient_data for missing coverage; watching when waiting for a performance/window or when adequate data does not meet demand thresholds; demand_supported when earlier/final demand and equivalent-time strength qualify; last_show_supported additionally requires post-performance strengthening. Each result stores settings/rule hash, reference event/site, measurement periods, inventory/price metrics, history revisions/timestamps, and evaluation time. The strongest qualifying reference/site wins; all evaluated references remain inspectable in stored evidence.

The worker checks bounded batches, skips unchanged input keys within the 15-minute recheck interval, and reevaluates on new history, membership revisions, rule changes, or time. History changes invalidate results; membership changes clear them. History revision checks and a generation/revision guard prevent an in-flight stale evaluation from overwriting invalidated results. Results expire after two recheck intervals so a stopped worker does not leave a supported signal visible indefinitely. Restart the patterns worker after deploying these changes.

## Later Tour Opportunity

This is a separate pattern from Last Show Effect. The series role maintains tour_opportunities after refreshing the existing event catalog; the patterns role runs a separate leased later-tour-opportunity job. The backend reads stored tour membership and per-target demand results through getConcertSeries?pattern=later_tour. Both patterns keep their own membership, results, invalidation generation and cache keys. The history worker uses tourTracked independently of seriesTracked so past opening shows remain eligible for refresh.

Membership is inferred, not verified by a tour ID: exact normalized tour title containing the word tour plus artist ID, at least two locations, with neighboring dates no more than 30 days apart. Names containing parking, premium, VIP, upgrade, package, bundle, pass, hospitality or soundcheck are excluded. Different tour titles are not merged. Incomplete source mappings, changed title spellings and long breaks can split or omit a real tour. No social-media metrics are collected and no social-hype claim is made.

The first three known performances are early references; dates after those are later targets. At least two completed references must hold same-market resale prices for 24 observed hours and show at least 20% primary OR resale inventory decline over their final seven days. Later dates up to 90 days ahead are considered. They need a seven-day (or since-reference-completion, if shorter) recent observation window, 24-hour price holding and at least 10% primary OR same-market resale decline. A later-tour-supported result also requires usable observations before the tour started and either a 5% resale-price increase or a 20% faster proportional inventory-depletion rate. Logarithmic depletion per observed hour makes rates comparable across window durations; steady proportional sales must not look accelerated just because the period is shorter. Current prices must also hold within 5% of their pre-tour median. A start-plus-three-hours timestamp approximates completion. Missing data is never filled with primary prices.

No absolute ticket price is compared across different cities. Equivalent-stage windows are retained as optional evidence using the same lead time to each show; incomplete long-range history is explicitly unavailable and does not become a price comparison. Settings use LATER_TOUR_* and the shared observation safeguards in LAST_SHOW_*; initial thresholds require historical validation. The UI exposes all tour groups by default, a status filter that matches any later target before pagination, early/later badges, per-event status, event/site chart navigation, extended 30/60/120-day axes, and the target's evidence. Only early references and currently eligible later targets are displayed/queued; complete tour membership remains stored for additions and cancellations.

Changed catalog membership invalidates tour results; history updates also invalidate them. Time/rule/history keys govern bounded reevaluation, with revision/generation guards on writes and expiry if workers stop. Start/restart the series, history and patterns roles (or npm start for all) and the backend to load this feature.
# Lower Resale and Higher Demand

The history role refreshes comparison metadata daily in batches of 50. The patterns role evaluates `lower-resale-demand` in batches of 10, independently from the existing detectors. Results live in `supply_change_events.resaleDemand`; no additional database is needed. Restart the history and patterns processes after deployment.

Upcoming events within 21 days have two possible routes:

- Primary sellout: an explicit fresh sold-out status, or a 25% primary decline across seven days ending with primary at 50 or below throughout the observed last day. All fresh available resale marketplaces must have 1–150 tickets. At least one must also show demand strengthening.
- Comparable events: at least three events with the same artist/tour or sports participants, market, weekday/weekend class, and venue (or documented capacities within 25%). Compare each marketplace separately at the same time before the event, using references within 90 days. Target resale must be below half the median of strong-demand references, with at least equivalent inventory decline or price growth.

Demand strengthening uses 48 hours of observations: resale inventory falls at least 10% while prices hold, or a price increase of at least 10% persists for 24 hours. Prices may fall no more than 5% below the baseline during the hold. Observations need at least four points, endpoints within 12 hours and no gaps above 12 hours. TM uses `resaleMinPrice` exclusively; zero resale counts are unavailable evidence. Inventory changes are demand proxies, not verified sales or social-media measurements.

The filter shows Supported, Watching, or Insufficient data, plus evidence and separate primary/resale chart lines. Results expire after 30 minutes, reevaluate every 15 minutes, and are invalidated by history or comparison metadata updates. Writes are guarded against concurrent changes. Comparisons use only retained history; missing references never become invented matches.

Optional `LOWER_RESALE_` settings: `WINDOW_DAYS` (7), `MAX_GAP_HOURS` (12), `MAX_COUNT` (150), `NEAR_PRIMARY` (50), `PRIMARY_DROP_PERCENT` (25), `COUNT_DROP_PERCENT` (10), `PRICE_RISE_PERCENT` (10), `PEER_RATIO` (0.5), `MIN_PEERS` (3), `BATCH_SIZE` (10).
